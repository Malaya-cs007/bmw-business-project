Business Challenge & Solution

The Problem:

Tracking production information and equipment malfunctions across disconnected departmental systems causes real-time visibility gaps and delays schedule adjustments during unexpected downtime.

The ERP Solution:

A unified relational database platform linking Equipment, Issues, Downtime, and Production Orders with role-based input workflows and automated duration calculation.

User Roles & Access Control (RBAC)

Standardized departmental inputs, operational visibility, and strict security isolation.

Production Line Supervisor

Line Operations & Monitoring

Monitors assembly line output, detects unexpected line stops, and logs initial technical disruption requests directly into the ERP system.

Key Responsibility:

Report interruptions immediately and coordinate safe resumption upon repair completion.

Data Entered / Changed

  • Production Order Status (e.g. Delayed)
  • Downtime Start Time timestamp
  • Reported Equipment Issue description

Information Viewed

  • Production schedules & order target dates
  • Equipment operational status
  • Active downtime logs on assigned line
Access Restriction Guardrail:

Supervisors cannot modify maintenance repair technical details or overwrite executive master scheduling priorities.

Maintenance Technician

Equipment Diagnostics & Repair

Investigates machine faults, performs mechanical/sensor maintenance, updates issue status, and logs repair timestamps.

Key Responsibility:

Execute repairs, verify safety protocols, and mark equipment ready for restart review.

Data Entered / Changed

  • Repair Status (In Progress / Resolved)
  • Technical Resolution Details
  • Downtime End Time timestamp

Information Viewed

  • Technical issues & reported error codes
  • Affected production order IDs
  • Equipment specifications & locations
Access Restriction Guardrail:

Technicians have full rights to equipment repair records but strict zero-permission to alter master vehicle order schedules or executive management notes.

Plant Manager

Executive Performance & Scheduling

Reviews holistic plant throughput, monitors downtime metrics, and adjusts vehicle model schedule priorities to maintain plant targets.

Key Responsibility:

Adjust high-level production schedules and reallocate capacity to mitigate systemic bottleneck delays.

Data Entered / Changed

  • Master Production Order Priorities
  • Scheduled Order Dates
  • Management Notes & Directive Flags

Information Viewed

  • Plant-wide downtime summaries
  • Performance analytics & bottleneck trends
  • Cross-departmental audit logs
Access Restriction Guardrail:

Plant managers adjust strategic dates and priorities, but database foreign keys prevent raw record deletions without verified DBA compliance.

System Evaluation & Security Governance

Analysis of data integrity guardrails, offline fallback procedures, and security controls.

1

How the ERP Solves Core Business Problems

By linking technical issues directly to equipment IDs, downtime records, and vehicle production orders in a single database, plant personnel gain real-time visibility into active disruptions. Plant managers can re-sequence order queues based on live equipment recovery times rather than relying on delayed paper reports.

2

Error Prevention & Constraints

Primary Keys ensure unique record identification, while Foreign Keys guarantee that downtime entries reference existing issues and orders. Input forms enforce timestamp logic (e.g., End Time must be greater than Start Time) and restrict status updates to valid pre-defined values.

3

System Offline & Network Fallback Protocol

If network connectivity is lost, plant personnel activate approved paper-based downtime tracking forms. Once system access is restored, designated ERP administrators back-enter timestamps, reconcile duplicate entries, and execute data validation checks. Daily automated database backups prevent data loss.

4

Security, Encryption & KPI Metrics

Individual user login credentials enforce Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. System performance is evaluated using key performance metrics: Mean Time to Detect (MTTD), Mean Time to Repair (MTTR), Schedule Adherence, and Record Accuracy.

Our Story

One Plant. Three Perspectives. A Shared Goal.

BMW Manufacturing's Spartanburg facility brings together people, technology, and production processes to build vehicles for customers in the United States and around the world. Its work connects manufacturing quality with efficient operations and coordinated business decisions.

Our group chose this business to explore a question: what does it take to keep a vehicle manufacturing plant running smoothly? We approached it through three connected careers: IT support, operations management, and financial analysis.

As a student team, our goal is to show how reliable technology, organized production, and informed financial planning work together to support a successful business.

This is an educational website created by students. The team below represents our assigned project roles, not BMW employees or founders.

How Our Roles Work Together

If a production system experiences a technical problem, IT support helps restore access. Operations management coordinates the production response and adjusts schedules as needed. Financial analysis examines how the interruption affects costs and budgets. Together, these roles help the business solve problems and meet its goals.
